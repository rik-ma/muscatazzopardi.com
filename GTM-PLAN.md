# Go-to-market plan — muscatazzopardi.com and the consulting practice

**Internal working document. July 2026. Not for publication.**

The plan in two sentences: the practice grows on referral and reputation, and the site exists to convert a mention into trust. Discovery happens in rooms (Vistage meetings, ICOM gatherings, peer referrals, podcast interviews), and the site is where a US owner who heard the name yesterday decides, inside twenty seconds, that Richard is the real thing.

## 1. Positioning

Richard is a strategy consultant and fractional CMO for owner-led manufacturing, technology, and professional services companies in the US and UK. He runs a deliberately small practice from Gozo, sells judgment (the ability to sit with an owner, cut through the reporting, and say "here's what this means, and here's what I'd do"), and publishes his working methods as open playbooks anyone can run on their own firm. The whole practice rests on a claim he has spent more than a decade proving at Switch: growth and kindness are not a trade-off.

**The Opposite Test.** A positioning plank only earns its place if a competent competitor could credibly claim the opposite. Run each one:

- *Deliberately small.* The opposite is a scaled fractional-CMO roster with bench coverage and account managers. Entire platforms run exactly that model. Passes.
- *Publishes methods openly.* The opposite is proprietary frameworks revealed only inside paid engagements. That is the default consulting posture. Passes.
- *Sells judgment.* The opposite is selling execution capacity by the hour. Most agencies live there. Switch sells execution alongside strategy, which is exactly why the personal practice must not. Passes.
- *Growth and kindness are not a trade-off.* The opposite (growth demands hard calls on people, and comfort is a cost) is held sincerely by half the operators Richard meets. Passes.
- *US and UK owner-led firms.* The opposite is serving everyone within reach, which is what most consultancies in a small market do. Passes.

Every plank excludes a real alternative, so the statement is a strategy. Apply the same test to any future copy before it ships.

## 2. Audience

Owner-led matters more than the industry label. The buyer is the person whose own money is at risk, who can decide alone, and who is buying a person, never a vendor. Manufacturing and technology owners in the US and UK fit because their firms are old enough to have a real commercial engine and exposed enough to commoditisation to feel it. Professional services firms fit for a sharper reason: AI is making their execution layer cheap, and the partners know it.

What they buy is clarity and judgment. These are people drowning in reports, dashboards, and advice; the thing they will pay a premium for is someone who knows their business, sits with them, and tells them what the situation means. They also buy safety. A strategy advisor with no execution business to upsell, no packages, and no lock-ins has visibly nothing to gain from the wrong recommendation, and owners can smell that within one conversation.

Where they look follows a fixed sequence. A name comes up in a trusted room: a Vistage group, an ICOM agency, an accountant or board advisor, the Dope CFO community, a former Northeastern student now running a P&L. Then they check LinkedIn. Then they land on the site and read for a minute or two, and the referral either hardens into an email or quietly dies. Increasingly there is a fourth step: they ask ChatGPT or Copilot who Richard Muscat Azzopardi is, which is what section 8 exists to handle. Nobody in this audience types "fractional CMO Malta" into Google, and the plan spends nothing on pretending otherwise.

## 3. The trust architecture

The site maps onto the Trust Equation from Maister, Green and Galford's *The Trusted Advisor*: trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) / self-orientation. Richard already teaches this to his Vistage group; the site should be the worked example.

| Component | The buyer's question | What carries it on the site |
|---|---|---|
| Credibility | Can I believe what he tells me? | The essays, each with at least one real number or story. About-page facts stated flat: Stanford GSB 2022, Northeastern since 2020, Vistage Chair since 2023, ICOM Strategic Committee. The 20-Point Commercial Analysis as named, defined method. |
| Reliability | Will he do what he says? | The Now page dateline, a public quarterly promise. A visible monthly essay rhythm with dates on everything. Contact copy calibrated to be keepable: "I read everything, and I reply to almost everything." |
| Intimacy | Do I feel safe with him? | First person all the way down. Rik as the sign-off. The Gozo house, the roof tiles, marathons run "with mixed grace," essays that open with what he got wrong first. |
| Self-orientation | Is he in this for himself? | The denominator, and the site's entire design answers it: no services page, no pricing, no testimonials, no funnels, essays that simply end, playbooks that give the method away, and exactly one commercial sentence on the whole site ("one new consulting engagement in the autumn, if it's the right one"). |

The insight from his own session applies here: self-orientation divides everything else. Most personal sites stack credibility signals and starve the other three components, then wonder why the credentials read as pitching. This site's restraint is the low-self-orientation play, executed in the architecture itself. Guard it. Any future proposal to add testimonials, client logos, or a booking widget fails this table before it fails taste.

## 4. Malta as an asset

Four moves, in order of priority.

**The time-zone line.** "Gozo is six hours ahead of New York — analysis in your inbox before your coffee." This reframes the only objection Malta raises (distance) as a head start: Richard works a full morning before a US client wakes, and deliverables land overnight from the client's perspective. Use the line in proposals, in the LinkedIn About section, and spoken on calls. On the site it belongs as future Contact-page texture, never as a hero claim.

**US-legible anchors first.** An American owner cannot place Malta, but places Vistage instantly (some 45,000 members worldwide, the bulk of them American), places Stanford GSB without help, and recognises Northeastern. Dope CFO adds a working proof: he already coaches US CFOs on US market realities every week. These anchors do the translation so that Malta reads as texture instead of risk. The skeleton already puts Stanford in the first ten seconds of the Home page; keep that discipline in every bio, podcast intro, and speaker one-sheet.

**Mediterranean texture second.** The planned photo essays (street and architecture photography, the Gozo build) give the site a warmth no US competitor can copy, and the design addendum already reserves the navigation slot. Sequence matters: anchor before texture. Texture without the anchors reads as a lifestyle blog; anchors without texture read as any consultant anywhere.

**Never sell Malta rates.** Price on the value of the judgment. Geography is a feature of the service, never a discount, and the first client who hears a cost-of-living justification will price every future recommendation the same way.

## 5. Essay pipeline

The Vistage chairing piece and "Buy it for life" from the original launch set move into the pipeline below. From August 2026, one essay a month, written in the provocation register of the Vistage sessions ("Every Yes Is a No"; "You are not busy. You are choosing this."). Rules carry over from the skeleton: 800 to 1,500 words, first person, at least one number or story per piece, no closing CTA.

1. **Every yes is a no** (Sep 2026). The session argument on paper: every commitment carries a hidden no, and naming that no is what separates a designed year from an accumulated one.
2. **Trust has a denominator** (Oct 2026). The Trust Equation, why self-orientation divides everything else you're good at, and the self-scores that surprise every room this gets run in.
3. **Everyone sounds the same** (Nov 2026). Put ten firms from one industry side by side and try to tell them apart; where the sameness comes from and the one-page fix.
4. **The diary is the strategy** (Dec 2026). Your calendar from last quarter is a more honest strategy document than the one in the board pack.
5. **Six hours ahead** (Jan 2027). Running a US advisory practice from a Gozo village, and what the distance does to the quality of the thinking.
6. **Your best client is subsidising your worst** (Feb 2027). Segment revenue by relationship value instead of service line and the firing decision argues itself.
7. **What's left when the report writes itself** (Mar 2027). AI is making execution cheap; what clients will still pay a premium for, and how to move a firm onto that side of the line.
8. **More clients would make me worse** (Apr 2027). Capacity as part of the service: why the practice stays small and what a full roster does to the quality of advice.
9. **Kindness has a P&L** (May 2027). The four-day week, the retention, the client tenure: a decade of numbers from running Switch kindly.
10. **Teach everything you know** (Jun 2027). Why the playbooks are public: the method was never the thing clients pay for, and holding it back costs more than giving it away.
11. **The house and the marathon** (Jul 2027). A slow build and long training blocks keep teaching the same lesson about patience, and I keep resenting it.
12. **The decision happens before the call** (Aug 2027). Buyers check you out in silence weeks before they write; what your public writing does while you sleep.

Sequencing logic: each playbook (section 6) gets its companion essay one month ahead of publication, and personal-texture pieces (5, 11) break up the practice pieces so the archive reads like a person. Swap titles freely; never skip a month twice in a row.

## 6. Playbook pipeline

One per quarter, published complete and usable. A playbook that holds the good part back is a brochure, and readers know it.

Two are live at launch: **"The one-person data team"** (the flagship: AI agents plus public data doing what used to need analysts — also his most shareable LinkedIn material, because the AI angle travels beyond the buyer audience) and **"Four tests for a positioning claim."** The pipeline from here:

1. **The trust equation self-audit** (Q4 2026). The four dimensions, the scoring questions from the session, a worked example showing why the denominator dominates, and what to do about a high self-orientation score. A reader runs it on their most important client relationship in an hour.
2. **Revenue segmentation and highest-LTV service mapping** (Q1 2027). Plot every service line by long-term relationship value on one axis and exposure to automation on the other; use the picture to decide where the next hire and the next marketing dollar go.
3. **The competitive positioning map** (Q2 2027). Pull the public language of every named competitor, map the claims, find the position nobody holds, and write your own to occupy it. This is the method behind the "everyone sounds the same" essay, handed over in full.
4. **Niche research conversations** (Q3 2027). Fifteen interviews across buyers, adjacent providers, and people who left the industry, with the exact questions, and how to turn the transcripts into a positioning decision.

The 20-Point Commercial Analysis gets separate treatment. Publish a definitive explainer page in autumn 2026 (what it is, the four areas it scores, what the gap between capability and perception looks like) so the term has one canonical home, and hold the full scored instrument back for now: it is the paid front door, and the explainer gives a reader enough to self-assess honestly. Revisit full publication once the term has taken hold; the transparency position argues for eventually opening it too.

## 7. Distribution

**LinkedIn.** Each essay gets a post within 48 hours of publishing: a 150–250 word excerpt that stands on its own, in the essay's own voice, with the link. The per-essay OG images (generated in the site build, design-system styled) do all the visual work; no carousels, no quote graphics. Between essays, two or three short posts a month in the same register: a provocation, or an anonymised observation from a session. Re-share each essay once, roughly six months later, with a new opening line. Pick ten people whose audiences are his buyers (Vistage speakers, manufacturing voices, professional-services advisors) and be a consistently useful presence in their comments.

**Newsletter.** The block says "New essays by email," and the send-only-when-written honesty is the differentiator: every competitor promises weekly and decays into silence or filler. Mechanics: signups land in Cloudflare KV today; sending goes through Resend Broadcasts (single opt-in per Richard's call, unsubscribe handled properly) and gets wired the first time there's an essay to send. No welcome sequence and no drip campaigns. The list is a room of people who asked to hear from him; treat it that way.

**ICOM.** The Strategic Committee seat is standing access to independent agency owners worldwide. Offer the playbooks as resources for member agencies, propose a session on escaping commoditisation at an ICOM meeting, and let agencies refer clients who need strategy without delivery. There is no conflict: the personal practice sells no execution, and where execution is needed Switch is adjacent.

**Vistage.** Two lanes. The warm one: his own group and fellow chairs are a natural referral network, and chairs are permanently hungry for session material; the playbooks are session material, so share them. The scale lane: apply to the Vistage speaker pool for the US and UK. One Vistage talk puts him in front of a room of exact-fit buyers with trust pre-installed, and "Every Yes Is a No" and the Trust Equation session are already speaker-grade assets.

**Guest lecturing.** Keep Northeastern; every cohort seeds future operators who remember the lecturer with the Maltese accent and the strong opinions. Accept one more recurring slot if it appears. Don't chase this surface; it matures on its own.

**Podcast guesting.** Shortlist criteria, applied strictly: the audience is owners and CEOs in manufacturing, technology, or professional services in the US or UK (never marketing-industry shows; those rooms are peers, not buyers); the format is long-form, 40 minutes or more, with a host who prepares; three past episodes confirm guests get substantive conversations; show notes carry links, so the site link lands. Pitch one a month with a specific essay or playbook as the proposed topic. A handful of right-fit appearances a year beats twenty generic ones.

## 8. GEO and LLM discoverability

When a referred buyer asks an assistant "who is Richard Muscat Azzopardi," the answer must be accurate, current, and sourced from his own pages.

- **llms.txt** at the site root: who he is, what the site holds, links to every essay and playbook in plain markdown. Cheap to maintain, and the crawlers that honour it feed exactly the answer engines this audience uses.
- **Schema.** JSON-LD Person on Home and About: name, alternateName "Rik," jobTitle, alumniOf Stanford GSB, affiliations (Switch, Vistage, ICOM), sameAs pointing at LinkedIn and switch.com.mt. BlogPosting on every essay with him as author. One spelling of the name everywhere.
- **Named-framework ownership.** "20-Point Commercial Analysis" gets one canonical page that defines it in the first hundred words, and the exact phrase gets used identically in essays, LinkedIn, proposals, and podcast intros. Language models attribute terms to whoever defines them most consistently in public. Give "Every Yes Is a No" the same treatment once the essay is live.
- **Index plumbing.** Bing Webmaster Tools on launch day (Bing's index feeds ChatGPT and Copilot answers), Google Search Console alongside it, the sitemap already handled by @astrojs/sitemap, IndexNow switched on through Cloudflare.
- **Quotable structure.** Write playbooks so a machine can lift them cleanly: the term defined at the top, steps as numbered lists, one plain summary sentence per section. This is also just good writing for a human in a hurry.

## 9. Measurement

Cloudflare Web Analytics only, by explicit choice: popularity data, no cookies, no consent banner, no marketing tracking. The monthly review takes twenty minutes and looks at four things: top pages (which essays get read informs the pipeline), referrers (a spike in direct traffic after a referral push means the validation loop is working), the US and UK share of visitors, and playbook views against essay views.

The number that matters lives off-platform. Keep a hand log, one line per inbound conversation: date, who, how they found him, what they wanted. Qualified inbound conversations per quarter is the practice's real KPI; everything Cloudflare shows is a leading indicator at best.

Ignore bounce rate and time on page (meaningless without the tracking he refuses to run), follower counts, rankings for generic terms like "fractional CMO," and anyone's engagement-rate spreadsheet. The dashboard is small because the honest one is small.

## The first 90 days (July–September 2026)

The site is live, indexed, and instrumented. The launch-month checklist (build, thesis essay, Search Console, Bing, analytics) is done. What follows is the traffic plan: slow, compounding, no paid anything. The principle is that the site converts attention it doesn't generate, so the 90 days are about putting the name and the ideas into rooms and feeds where the buyers already are, one small repeatable action at a time.

### The weekly rhythm (about 90 minutes, total)

- **One LinkedIn post a week.** Rotate three types: an essay or playbook excerpt with the link (the OG card does the visual work), a provocation in the Vistage register, an anonymised observation from a session or engagement. Written like the essays: first person, one specific number or story, no hashtag salad.
- **One useful comment a day, ten minutes.** On the ten-person list (section 7): Vistage speakers, manufacturing and professional-services voices whose audiences are the buyers. Substance only; a comment that could only come from someone who does the work.
- **The inbound log, as things happen.** One line per inbound conversation in `C:\Claude\RMA\INBOUND-LOG.md`: date, who, how they found him, what they wanted. This is the KPI; everything else is weather.

### July — seed the network

1. **Profile plumbing, one sitting:** LinkedIn website field → muscatazzopardi.com; LinkedIn About section rewritten in the site's register with the time-zone line; email signature link; Switch team page links to the personal site; Vistage chair profile and Dope CFO bio carry the URL. Every one of these is a permanent drip of exactly-right visitors, and they also feed the entity consolidation in section 8.
2. **The quiet launch post.** Not "I built a website": post the kindness thesis as an idea, with the essay link carrying it. The site should be discovered mid-thought.
3. **Playbooks into the network.** Send "The one-person data team" personally to a handful of fellow Vistage chairs and ICOM contacts as something their members/agencies can use — a gift, not a broadcast.
4. **Repo housekeeping:** the GitHub repo goes private (it holds this plan and the unpublished instrument).

### August — establish the rhythm

1. **Essay four ships** ("Every yes is a no" pulled forward, or the Vistage chairing piece — whichever is further along in his head), with its LinkedIn excerpt inside 48 hours.
2. **First podcast pitch** per the section 7 criteria, proposing the one-person data team as the topic (the AI angle gets bookings right now).
3. **Second LinkedIn pass on the flagship playbook** with a different opening — the certification-map example instead of the CRM example.

### September — first review, first sends

1. **Essay five ships**, same drill.
2. **Wire Resend Broadcasts** and send the first newsletter issue to whatever the list holds by then, however small. The promise is "when they're written," so the habit starts with the first one written after the list existed.
3. **First monthly metrics ritual** (section 9): twenty minutes on Cloudflare Web Analytics + Search Console queries + the inbound log. This sets the baseline the slow-but-surely gets measured against.
4. **Second podcast pitch.** One a month, forever, is the pace.
5. **Draft the 20-Point explainer** for its October slot.

### What "working" looks like at day 90

Not traffic numbers. Working is: six-plus LinkedIn posts published on schedule, five essays live, the profile plumbing all pointing home, one podcast recorded or booked, the first newsletter sent, and at least one line in the inbound log that traces back to something published rather than someone met. If the log stays empty of published-source entries at day 90, the content is fine and the distribution is under-powered: double the network sends before changing anything on the site.
